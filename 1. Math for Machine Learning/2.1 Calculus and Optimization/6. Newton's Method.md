The Newton's method help us in finding zero of polynomial by iterative approach

![](assets/newton's%20method/newton's%20method.svg)

Here for a given polynomial we find slope. We can find value of slope by derivative and initially give a random $x_{0}$ value, using the above equation we can find value of $x_{1}$. Now $x_{1}$ becomes our new point where we find the slope. We continue the method till we find the zero of polynomial.

Here if we have,

$$
	\begin{align}
	f(x)&=x^2-2x-8 \\
	f'(x)&=2x-2 \\
	\text{When solved analytically we} &\text{ find zero of polynomial are -2 and 4}
	\end{align}
$$


```python
X_b = 11
for i in range(9):
    x_s = X_b - ((X_b**2-2*X_b-8)/(2*X_b-2))
    print(f"Iteration {i} value of X_s : {x_s}")
    X_b=x_s
```

Newton's method only find local zero of polynomial( roots of eq.). Hence to find both the roots we can take starting point to be a list of array having random starting points and run the iterations. For the above iteration if $X_b=-5$. It give $-2$ as output.
## Newton's Method for Optimization (finding minima)

Up until now we have used gradient descent to find the minima

$$
	\begin{align}
	f(x)&=x^2-2x-8 \\
	f'(x)&=2x-2 \\ 
	\text{Solving,} \\
	0&=2x-2 \\ 
	x&=1 \\
	\text{we find minima occurs at x=1}
	\end{align}
$$

```python
alpha = 0.1
x_old = 9
for i in range(15):
    x_new = x_old - alpha*(2*x_old-2)
    print(f"Iteration {i} value of X_new : {x_new}")
    x_old = x_new
```

Now we know Newton's method help us in finding the zero of polynomial. In finding minima we first derivate the polynomial and find the zero of derivated polynomial. Hence we can apply the Newton's method on first derivative of a polynomial to find the minima.

$$
	\begin{align}
	f(x)&=x^2-2x-8 \\
	f'(x)&=2x-2 \\
	f''(x)&=2 \\
	\text{Applying Newton's Method in }f'(x), \\
	x_{1}=x_{0}-\frac{f'(x)}{f''(x)}
	\end{align}
$$

```python
X_b = 11
for i in range(9):
    x_s = X_b - ((2*X_b-2)/(2))
    print(f"Iteration {i} value of X_s : {x_s}")
    X_b=x_s

output :
```

Here we can see Newton's method is much more accurate and faster than gradient descent. 

Even though Newton's method is much faster and accurate, in machine learning it is rarely used due to at higher dimension (no. of weights increased) we require creation of inverse of Hessian matrix which is very costly computationally. In gradient descent we simply need to compute gradient vectors.

## Second Derivative

Lets take an example of a car moving and coming back

![](assets/newton's%20method/first%20derv%20graph.svg)

Here Initially the car speeds up, moves at constant speed then slows down and move at constant speed.

Now graph of second derivative i.e, derivative of velocity (which is acceleration)

![](assets/newton's%20method/acc%20graph.svg)

- $1st$ section for constant positive slope , derivative is a constant. 
- $2nd$ section as velocity is constant. Its derivative is 0.
- $3rd$ section as velocity is decreasing i.e., negative slope. The derivative is a negative value.
- $4th$ section again as velocity is constant. Its derivative is 0.
Now, comparing the first distance-time graph and acceleration graph :

![](assets/newton's%20method/second%20derv.svg)

Here :
- $1st$ section, as the slope starts to rise from straight line (positive slope ahead), the second derivative is a positive value.
- $2nd$ section, slope is constant , second derivative is 0.
- $3rd$ section, slope becomes zero at some point and starts to decrease (negative slope ahead), the second derivative is negative value.
- $4th$ section, again as slope is constant, second derivative is 0.

>[!Note]
>Second derivative means the change in curvature of a graph
>1. If slope starts to rise (positive slope ahead), second derivative give positive value.
>2. If slope starts to decrease (negative slope ahead), second derivative give negative value.
>
>Second derivative helps us in knowing whether the extrema we found is a maxima or minima
>1. If second derivative is positive, we found local minima.
>2. If second derivative is negative, we found local maxima
