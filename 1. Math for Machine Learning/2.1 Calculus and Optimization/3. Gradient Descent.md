## Gradient Descent in one variable

![](assets/gradient%20descent/min%20in%20one%20variable.svg)

solving $e^x=\frac{1}{x}$ is quite complex in computer.
![](assets/gradient%20descent/min%20by%20jump.svg)

Here we choose a random point on the curve and increase the x by some constant amount on left and right. To get minima we move towards the lesser side . After few iterations we get a point that is close to minima. Here we didn't need to differentiate the function.  
The issue in this method is since we are jumping by some constant value if, the jump is high we may get huge error. 

### Optimization

Instead of jumping by a constant value we look at the slope.
- If the random starting point is towards left of minima, we get negative slope and to move towards minima we need to move right i.e., increase $x + slope$ value. Here as we approach towards minima the slope value decrease automatically decreasing the jump.
- If the random starting point is towards right of minima, we get positive slope and to move towards minima we need to move left i.e., decrease $x - slope$ value. Here as we approach towards minima the slope value decrease automatically decreasing the jump.

$$
 \begin{align}
 \text{New point = old point - slope} \\
 \end{align}
$$

Here if random starting point is to left of minima, it give negative slope putting it in equation result in 

$$
\begin{align}
	\text{New Point = old point - (-slope)} \\
	\text{New Point = old point + slope} \\
	\text{moving new point towads right}
\end{align}
$$

if starting point is in right of minima, it give positive slope,

$$
	\begin{align}
	\text{New Point = old point - slope} \\
	\text{moving new point towards left} 
	\end{align}
$$

## Drawback of gradient descent

![](assets/gradient%20descent/drawback_1.svg)

The main drawback of gradient descent is that if the eq has multiple minima it can get stuck in local minima. There is no mathematical way to find the global minima hence we have to take random multiple starting point and see if we can find better minima.

## Gradient Descent with two variable

We know a partial derivative of $f(x,y)$ w.r.t $y$ is simply a derivative of the function by keeping $x$ at some constant.

![](assets/gradient%20descent/partial%20derivative%20wrt%20y.svg)

and when we do w.r.t $x$ it simply mean derivative of $f(x,y)$ by keeping $y$ constant

![](assets/gradient%20descent/partial%20derivative%20wrt%20x.svg)

The resultant gradient vector 

$$
	\nabla z=\begin{bmatrix}
	\frac{df}{dx} \\
	\frac{df}{dy}
	\end{bmatrix}
$$

is simply a vector that always points towards steepest ascend and the amount by which it ascend is the magnitude of vector $\nabla z$.

![](assets/gradient%20descent/gradient%20vector.svg)

Our goal is to find the direction of steepest descent hence we simply go in direction opposite to gradient vector $-\nabla z$.
Hence ,

$$
	(x_{new},y_{new})=(x_{old}, y_{old})-\alpha(\nabla z)
$$
