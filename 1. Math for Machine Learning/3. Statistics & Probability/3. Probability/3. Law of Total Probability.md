Q. In a bucket containing 4 red and 3 white balls, 2 balls are drawn at random one at a time without replacement.
- A : Event of drawing a red ball in 1st attempt.
- B : Event of drawing a red ball in 2nd attempt.
What is the probability of event B occurring ? 
Ans : 
- Here Event B is dependent on event A. 
- If event A is success we have 1 less red ball decreasing the probability of getting red ball again the second time i.e., event B.
- If event A is failure all red ball are still in bucket and 1 white ball is less, increasing the probability of getting red ball the second time i.e., event B.
$$
\begin{align}
P(B)&=P(A\cap B) + P(\bar{A}\cap B) \\
 \\
&=P(A).P(B|A) + P(\bar{A}).P(B|\bar{A}) \\
 \\
&=\frac{4}{7}.\left( \frac{3}{6} \right)+\frac{3}{7}.\left( \frac{4}{6} \right)
\end{align}
$$
## Definition

If $B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}\dots$ is a partition of sample space S, then for any event A we have,
$$
	P(A)=\sum_{i}P(A\cap B_{i})=\sum P(B_{i}).P(A|B_{i})
$$
If we see the Venn diagram

![](../assets/2.%20Probability/total%20probability.svg)

- Since $B_{1},B_{2},B_{3}$ are partitions of sample space S,
	$S=U_{i}B_{i}$ i.e., Union of $B_{i}$
	we can also write,
	$$
	\begin{align}
	A&=A\cap S \\
	
	&=A\cap(U_{i}B_{i}) \\
	
	&=U_{i}(A\cap B_{i})\text{ [ by distributive law]} \\

	\text{Applying Probability,} \\

	P(A)&=P(U_{i}(A\cap B_{i})) \\
	&=\sum P(A\cap B_{i})
	\end{align}
	$$
	
This is called Law of total Probability.

Q. I have 3 bags that contain 100 marbles each
- Bag_1 contain 75 red and 25 blue marbles.
- Bag_2 contain 60 red and 40 blue marbles.
- Bag_3 contain 45 red and 55 blue marbles.
If I randomly choose a bag and pick a marbles. What is the probability that the marble is red?

Ans : Here the probability of picking red marble depend on which bag i choose. So,
-  $B_{i}$ = event that bag $B_{i}$ is chosen.
- $R$ = event that chosen marble is red.
$$
	\begin{align}
	P(R) &= P(B_{1}\cap R)+P(B_{2}\cap R)+P(B_{3}\cap R) \\
	 \\
	&= P(B_{1}).P(R|B_{1})+P(B_{2}).P(R|B_{2})+P(B_{3}).P(R|B_{3}) \\
	 \\
	&=\frac{1}{3}.\left( \frac{75}{100} \right)+\frac{1}{3}.\left( \frac{60}{100} \right)+\frac{1}{3}.\left( \frac{45}{100} \right)
	\end{align}
$$
