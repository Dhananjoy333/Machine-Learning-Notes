Probability is defined as the likelihood of the occurrence of any event. For example : How likely it will rain today ?.

## Random Experiment

Experiment for which we don't know the outcome but we know the range of all possible outcome. Example : Tossing a coin, rolling a dice.

## Sample Space (S) 

The set of all possible outcome of an experiment.
Example : 
1. S = {H, T} ---> for 1 coin toss
2. S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6} ---> for rolling a die
3. S = {HT, TH, HH, TT} ---> for 2 coin toss

Sample space is also called Domain Set. Sometimes, we may even have continues sample space.
Example : Finding the lifespan of car. S = $[0,\infty)$

## Event

Subset of a sample space. 
Example : 
1. Rolling 1 dice and getting even number.
		E = {2, 4, 6}
2. Tossing 2 coins and getting exactly 1 head.\
		E = {HT, TH}
Both events and samples are set.

>[!Note]
>If the sample space of an experiment is n. Then the number of possible events is
>$$
>	\text{no. of possible Events(E)} = 2^n
>$$

Let S be the sample space and E be the Event space. Then the Probability of occurrence of Event E is
$$
\begin{align}
P(E) &= \frac{n(E)}{n(S)} \\ \\

	P(E)&=\frac{\text{no. of favorable outcomes}}{\text{total no. of possible outcomes}} \\ \\
	
	0\leq &P(E)\leq 1
\end{align}
$$

Q. If 2 coins are tossed simultaneously, what is the probability of getting at least 1 head ?
Ans : 
- Sample space (S) = {HT, HH, TH, TT}
- Event(E)/ favorable outcomes = {HT, TH, HH}
- So,
$$
P(E)=\frac{n(E)}{n(S)}=\frac{3}{4}=0.75
$$

## Notation

Let A and B be two events of a random experiment.
- $P(A\cap B)=P(A\text{ and }B)=P(AB)=$ Probability of occurring both event A and B.
- $\cap$ is called intersection. So Probability of A intersection B.
- $P(A\cup B)=P(A \text{ OR }B)=$ Probability of occurring of event A or B.
- $\cup$ is called Union. So Probability of A union B.

## Mutually Exclusive Events

Two event are mutually exclusive if they cannot happen at the same time.

$$
\begin{align}
\text{Formally, } A\cap B&=\varnothing \\ \\

\text{It means, }P(A\cap B)&=0 
\end{align}
$$

Example :
- Rolling a die --> random experiment
	- S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}
	- $E_{1}$ = Getting an even no. = {2, 4, 6}
	- $E_{2}$ = Getting an odd no. = {1, 3, 5}
	- $E_{3}$ = Getting a prime no. = {2, 3, 5}

## Probability Axioms

Probability Theory is based on some axioms that acts as the foundation for the theory.
*Axiom is assumption that is accepted as true*.

- Axiom - 1 : Probability of any event is never negative. $P(A)\geq 0$, where A is any event.
- Axiom - 2 : The probability of entire sample is 1. $P(S)=1$.
- Axiom - 3 : If $A_{1},A_{2}$ are disjoint/mutually exclusive events. $P(A_{1}\cup A_{2})=P(A_{1})+P(A_{2})$.
- Axiom - 4 : If $A_{1},A_{2}$ are not mutually exclusive events. $P(A_{1}\cup A_{2})=P(A_{1})+P(A_{2})-P(A\cap B)$.

We can also generalize Axiom - 3 to n mutually exclusive event.

$$
	P(A_{1}\cup A_{2}\cup A_{3}\cup\dots A_{n})=P(A_{1})+P(A_{2})\dots P(A_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^nP(E_{i})
$$
## Independent and Dependent Events

### Independent Events

Independent Event are events that are not affected by occurrence of other events.

$$
	P(A\cap B)=P(A).P(B)
$$

Example 1 : Tossing a coin 2 times. OR Tossing 2 coins at once.
- Sample Space = {HH, HT, TH, TT}
- A = Getting a Head in first toss = {HH, HT}
- B = Getting a Tail in second toss = {HT, TT}
What is the probability both event will be true.
Ans : 
Both event will be true if coins show { HT }.
$$
	\begin{align} \\
	P(A) &= \frac{2}{4} \\
	 \\
	P(B) &= \frac{2}{4} \\
	 \\
	P(A\cap B)&=\frac{1}{4} \\
	 \\
	
	P(A\cap B)&=P(A)*P(B)=\frac{2}{4}*{\frac{2}{4}}=\frac{1}{4}
	\end{align}
$$
- Here Before any one of the experiment occurred the individual probability was $P(A)=\frac{2}{4}$ and $P(B)=\frac{2}{4}$ .
- After one event A occurred i.e., tossed the coin once individual probability are still $P(A)=\frac{2}{4}$ and $P(B)=\frac{2}{4}$.
- Occurrence of any one event doesn't change the outcome of other event.

### Dependent Events

Two event are dependent if occurrence of one changes the probability of other.
$$
	P(A\cap B)=P(A).P(B|A)
$$
>[!Note]
here $P(B|A)$ is probability of B given A has already occurred. This is called Conditional Probability.  Also $P(B|\bar{A})$ is probability of B given A was failure.

Example 1 : Drawing cards without replacement.
- A = First card drawn is Ace.
- B = Second card drawn is Ace.
Ans :
Here when we first draw a card probability of being ace is $P(A)=\frac{4}{52}$. 
Since we are not replacing if we get ace now we have only 51 cards (1 less ace). So, when we try to pick another card, given event A was success $P(B|A)=\frac{3}{51}$. 
$$
	\begin{align}
	P(A)&=\frac{4}{52} \\ \\
	P(B|A)&=\frac{3}{51} \\
	 \\ 
	 P( B|\bar{A})&=\frac{4}{51} \\
	  \\
	 
	P(A\cap B)=P(A)*P(B|A)&=\frac{4}{52}*\left( \frac{3}{51} \right)
	\end{align}
$$

- Here before any event occurred i.e., before the card was pulled. Individual probabilities of events were $P(A)=\frac{4}{52}$ and $P(B)=\frac{4}{52}$.
- But after event A happened as we don't put the card back in. The individual probability are $P(A)=\frac{4}{52}$, now if A is success we have 1 less card and that is Ace. Hence probability of $P(B)=\frac{3}{51}$.
- After one of the event happened it influenced the outcome of other hence events are dependent.

Here we are pulling the card 2 times causing the dependency i.e., 2 actions. But it doesn't necessarily have to involve two action

Example 2 : Rolling a dice.
- Sample Space = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}
- A = number is even = {2, 4, 6}
- B = number is greater than 3 = {4, 5, 6}
Ans :
Here Before the dice is rolled,
$$
	\begin{align}
	P(A)=\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2} \\
	
 P(B)=\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2}
	\end{align}
$$
Now after the dice is rolled lets say event A is success, now possible outcomes are {2,4,6}. For event B to also be success the numbers must be {4, 6}. So, if event A is success $P(B|A)=\frac{2}{3}$
$$
	\begin{align}
	P(A)=\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2} \\
	\text{given knowledge that event A is success} \\
	P(B|A)=\frac{2}{3}
	\end{align}
$$
- Here probability of event B changed by knowing that event A was success. Hence both events are dependent.

>[!Formulae]
>1. If A and B are Mutually Exclusive.
>   $$
> \begin{align}
>	A\cap B&=\varnothing \\
>	P(A\cap B)&=0 \\
>	P(A\cup B)&=P(A)+P(B)
>\end{align}
>   $$
>   
>   2. If A and B are dependent events.
>   $$
>	\begin{align}
>	P(A\cap B)=P(A).P(B|A)
>	\end{align}
>   $$
>   
>   3. If A and B are Independent events. 
>   $$
>	\begin{align}
>	P(B|A)&=P(B) \\
>	P(A\cap B)&=P(A).P(B)
>	\end{align}
>   $$
