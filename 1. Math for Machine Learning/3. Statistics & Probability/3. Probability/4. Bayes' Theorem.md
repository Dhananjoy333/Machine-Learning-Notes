Lab proposed a test for a rare disease which is 99% effective i.e., the test works 99% of the time for that disease.
- The rare disease affects $\frac{1}{10,000}$ i.e., 1 out of 10,000 people.
- The population of the country is 1M people.

Analysis of statements:
1. Lab test is 99% accurate.

|                      | diagnosed sick | diagnosed healthy |
| -------------------- | -------------- | ----------------- |
| 100 Healthy people   | 1              | 99                |
| 100 unhealthy people | 99             | 1                 |
In machine learning we call this ***Confusion Matrix.

2. From point 1 and 2 

![](../assets/2.%20Probability/test%20diagnosed.svg)

|                  | diagnosed sick | diagnosed healthy |
| ---------------- | -------------- | ----------------- |
| Actually Healthy | 9,999          | 989,901           |
| Actually Sick    | 99             | 1                 |
- Here the total no. of people whom the test has diagnosed as sick is = 9,999  + 99 = 10,098
So, 

Q. What is the probability that the person that was diagnosed as sick by the test is actually sick ?

Ans :
- Diagnosed as sick that are actually sick = 99
- Total diagnosed as sick = 10,098
$$
\begin{align}
P(sick|\text{diagnosed sick})&= \frac{99}{10,098} \\
&=0.0098
\end{align}
$$
Conclusion, even though the test show an accuracy of 99%. If we do the test and is diagnosed as sick by the test there is actually less than 1% (0.98%) chance that you are actually sick.

This is called ***Base Rate Fallacy or False Positive Paradox***. Here even if test has 99% accuracy. Its ability to actually diagnosed correctly is less than 1%. This happening due to rarity of disease and large population. Here even an error of 1% cause huge miscalculation.

We have solved the question using Confusion matrix now we solve with Bayes' Theorem Formula.

## Bayes' Theorem Formula

- 1 out of 10,000 is affected/sick. i.e.,
$$
\begin{align}
sick &= \frac{1}{10000}*100=0.01\% \\ \\

P(\text{sick})&=0.01\% \\ \\

\text{So} \\
P(\text{not sick})&= 99.99\%
\end{align}
$$
- The test is 99% accurate
$$
\begin{align}
P(\text{diagnosed sick | sick})&=99\% \\
 \\
P(\text{diagnosed sick | not sick})&=1\%
\end{align}
$$
Find $P(\text{sick | diagnosed sick})$

Ans : 
- A : sick.
- B : diagnosed sick.
$$
\begin{align}
P(A|B)&=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)} \\
 \\
\text{Now, from Conditional Probaility,} \\
 \\
P(A\cap B)&=P(A).P(B|A) \\
&=\frac{0.01}{100}*\left( \frac{99}{100} \right) \\
 \\
\text{From Law of Total Probability,} \\
 \\
P(B) &= P(B\cap A) +P(B\cap\bar{A}) \\
&=P(A).P(B|A)+P(\bar{A}).P(B|\bar{A}) \\
&=\frac{0.01}{100}.\left( \frac{99}{100} \right)+\frac{99.99}{100}.\left( \frac{1}{100} \right) \\
 \\
\text{Substituting the values,} \\
P(A|B) = 0.0098
\end{align}
$$

>[!Formula]
>In general we can write
>$$
>	P(A|B) = \frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(A\cap B)+P(\bar{A}\cap B)}
>$$

## Prior and Posterior Probability

In the previous experiment we saw,
1. A test for a rare disease has an accuracy of 99%.$$
		P(\text{diagnosed sick | sick})= 99%
	$$
2. But if disease is very rare ($\frac{1}{10000}$ people).
	- Most positive test result might still be false alarm.
3. we proved using Bayes Theorem. $P(\text{sick | diagnosed sick})<1\%$.
$$
	P(\text{sick | diagnosed sick})= \frac{P(\text{sick }\cap \text{ diagnosed sick})}{P(\text{diagnosed sick})}
$$

Priors : Initial belief --> $P(\text{sick})$. i.e., 1 out of 10000 people.
Posterior : updated belied --> $P(\text{sick | diagnosed sick})$. i.e., less than 1% chance.

Bayes' Theorem converts the result from test into real probability of the event . We can 
- Correct for measurement errors.
- Relate the actual probability to measured test probability.
That is :
- P(sick | diagnosed sick) from P(diagnosed sick | sick).
- The test claims P(diagnosed sick | sick) i.e., probability that actual sick will be diagnosed as sick is 99%.
- But in reality Bayes theorem show P(sick | diagnosed sick) i.e., probability of diagnosed sick to be actually sick is less than 1%. Hence 99% of the time diagnosed person is not actually sick which is correction of initial belief (prior) of 99% being sick.
