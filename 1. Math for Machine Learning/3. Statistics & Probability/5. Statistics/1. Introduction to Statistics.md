Statistics is the science of collecting, analyzing, interpreting and presenting data.
- Purpose : Helps us understand patterns in real world data and make inferences about population from samples.
- Example : A dice is rolled 1000 times and outcome is noted. What does it tell us about the Dice ? Is it biased or unbiased ?.

Probability is the branch of mathematics that studies randomness and uncertainty.
- Purpose : what is the chance of something happening?
- Example : Given a fair dice. Find probability of getting an even no.?

|Probability|Statistics|
|---|---|
|Starts with a known model or data distribution|Starts with collected data|
|Predicts what is likely to happen|Infers what is true about the population|
|Future-oriented|Past/present-oriented|
|Uses known information to find unknown outcomes|Uses observed outcomes to estimate unknown information|
## Real-life example

Imagine a factory making light bulbs.
### Probability

The company knows from years of testing that **2% of bulbs are defective**.
Question:
If I buy one bulb, what is the chance it's defective?

Answer:
$$
	P(defective)=0.02
$$
Known probability → predict an outcome.

---
### Statistics

Now imagine you don't know the defect rate.
You randomly inspect **500 bulbs** and find **12 defective**.

You estimate:
$$
	\frac{12}{500}=0.024
$$
So you conclude the factory's defect rate is **about 2.4%**.
Observed data → estimate an unknown probability.

## Types of Statistics

1. **Descriptive statistics= Describe the data you have.**  
2. **Inferential statistics= Infer (guess/estimate) something about a larger population from the data you have.**
## 1. Descriptive Statistics

Descriptive statistics **summarizes and organizes data**. It does **not** make predictions or conclusions beyond the data collected. It is divided into :

![](descriptive_stats.svg)
### Example

Suppose a class has marks:

```
60, 65, 70, 75, 80
```

Descriptive statistics answers questions like:
- What is the average mark?
- What is the highest mark?
- How spread out are the marks?

It calculates things such as:
- Mean
- Median
- Mode
- Range
- Variance
- Standard deviation
- Charts (histograms, bar charts, box plots)
It only **describes these 5 students**.
## 2. Inferential Statistics

Inferential statistics uses a **sample** to draw conclusions about the **entire population**.
### Example

A university has **20,000 students**.
Instead of asking every student, you survey **500 students**.
Suppose their average height is **170 cm**.

You infer:

> "The average height of all 20,000 students is probably around 170 cm."
