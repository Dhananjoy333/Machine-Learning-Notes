In all of our examples we have already known the standard deviation but in real case we won't always know the standard deviation. We know if we don't have the population standard deviation we can use the standard deviation of the sample, but doing so changes our distribution to Student's t-distribution

![](../assets/Hypothesis%20testing_2/t-test.svg)

We know t-distribution is dependent on degree of freedom,

![](../assets/Hypothesis%20testing_2/t-test_3.svg)

Higher the degree of freedom $\nu$, more it resembles normal distribution. at high enough $\nu$ it align perfectly with normal distribution when the sample size is about 30, this is why taking high sample size is important if standard deviation is unknown and if we want to use sample standard deviation. In our case we only have 10 sample size.

![](../assets/Hypothesis%20testing_2/t-test_1.svg)

Now coming back to our 3 tests :

![](../assets/Hypothesis%20testing_2/t-test_2.svg)

We have a sample size of 10 hence degree of freedom is 9, if we look at the t-distribution for $\nu=9$

![](../assets/Hypothesis%20testing_2/t-test_4.svg)

Here we see the top of t-distribution does not match the top of normal distribution, but in both case area under the curve remains 1 (area under the curve = probability). Hence to maintain the area its a bit fatter.

![](../assets/Hypothesis%20testing_2/t-test_5.svg)

We can see here 
- when we were using the known population standard deviation of 3, the our right tailed test was getting rejected at $\alpha=0.05$ but here it has changed now it is not rejecting $H_{o}$ for same values.
