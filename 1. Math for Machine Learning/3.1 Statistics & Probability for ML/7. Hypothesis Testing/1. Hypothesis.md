Hypothesis testing evaluates sample data to see if there is enough evidence to reject the **null hypothesis** $(H_{o})$ in favor of the **alternative hypothesis** $(H_{a})$. It specifically tests the strength of the evidence against the null hypothesis rather than proving one side absolutely.

Example :
In testing whether  email is spam or ham, it is much suitable to have some spam email categorized as ham than some ham email simply being deleted due to wrong spam categorization.  

![](../assets/Hypothesis%20testing/hypothesis.svg)

Hence,
- Initially all email is categorized as ham, this is called **Null Hypothesis ($H_o$)**. It is a point where no assumptions were made.
- Now we look at some evidence inside the email like keywords, sender, email length etc. to determine if we have enough evidence to proof the email is not actually ham.
- Once enough evidence is stacked against email being ham, then the email is categorized as spam. This is called **Alternate Hypothesis $(H_a)$**. 
- Here, emails being ham or spam is mutually exclusive i.e., email will always be categorized as one or the other. An email cannot be spam and ham at the same time.
- Asking whether email is ham or spam will have answer is True or False.
