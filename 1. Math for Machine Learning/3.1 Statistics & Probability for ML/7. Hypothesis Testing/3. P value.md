Previously we took height mean of sample size 10 of students in US who are 18 y/o which we got to be 68.442. We also had the population mean of height of 18 y/o from the 70s as 66.7. Now we ask the question : did the average height increased over the years? Here the alternate hypothesis is greater than null hypothesis hence it is a right tailed test.

![](../assets/Hypothesis%20testing/p%20val.svg)

Now looking at the values of sample mean and population mean we can simply assume because sample mean is higher, height has increased but direct conclusion as such could cause Type 1 error (we concluded average height has increased over the year, in reality it has not). So, the next question is : given the the sample value should be reject null hypothesis or not? or is the sample mean statistically large enough to be significant?
- If the sample mean was 90 which is much higher than 66.7, with high confidence we can say that average height has indeed increased, highly decreasing the chances of Type 1 error.
- But if the sample mean was 66.8 which is very close to 66.8, we would not be too confident to sat the average height has increased, and if we did say it the chances of Type 1 error would be very high for this statistical value.
- Hence we need a way to determine whether for a given sample mean, can we safely say whether the average height is increased or not. This is where **P value** helps us.   
Before answering the question, we set a significance level $\alpha=0.05$ we are willing to accept 5% probability of Type 1 error. Now if we assume that 68.442 is significant enough to be able to reject null hypothesis then all values greater than 68.442 must also be significant enough to be able to reject null hypothesis. Hence we find probability of $\bar{X}$ greater than 68.442 given that mean is 66.7, which is basically the area under the curve of normal distribution with mean 66.7 and standard deviation of 3 (given) in the interval greater than 68.442. If the calculated probability is less than significance level i.e., less than the risk we are willing to take than we can safely reject the null hypothesis. This probability which gets compared to significance level is called **P value** (way to calculate p-value in later notes).

## Calculating p value with Z statistics

We know a z-score tells you how many standard deviations a specific data point is away from the mean. It is given by

$$
\begin{align}
Z&=\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma} \\
\text{Because our mean is not an individual }& \text{value rather a sample mean of sample size n,} \\
Z&=\frac{\bar{X}-\mu}{\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{ n }}}
\end{align}
$$
