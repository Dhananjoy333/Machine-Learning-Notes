When our events are a list for ex : if we toss coin 3 times. $X:$ number of head. Value of $X$ can be $0,1,2,3$ . It cannot have a decimal value and it can form a list. Whereas an event such as $X:$ amount of time waiting for bus. Here cannot say the exact value like $1min$ because it can also be $1min$ $2sec$ or $1min$ $2sec$ $4nanosec$ , i.e., there is no fixed value instead it can be an interval like between 1-2min. 

Hence, a distribution is discrete if the random variable is a list and a distribution is  continuous if random variable is an interval.

In continuous distribution the probability at an exact moment cannot be determined. For ex : the probability that a call take exactly 1 min. Here the call must end at exactly one minute not 0.99999... or 1.0000...1 for the probability to exist, which is impossible hence probability at exact moment is 0.

We can represent the case in terms of intervals and graph the probability. Here the total of all height is 1.

If we keep decreasing the interval eventually we simply get a curve whose area under the curve is 1

## Probability Density Function (PDF)

In discrete distribution we had exact probability value for each value of random variable and we represented it with the help of Probability Mass Function (PMF). But in continuous distribution we cannot determine the probability at an exact moment. Instead we have intervals :

![](assets/cont.%20prob/pdf%20ex1.svg)

Here we know the sum of all heights (area under rectangle) is 1 which is divided into 5 equal parts. Hence,

$$
	P(\text{between 2 and 3})=\frac{1}{5}
$$

If we take previous example where probabilities are not constant

![](assets/cont.%20prob/pdf%20ex2.svg)

Here we can see the $P(\text{between 1 and 2 min})$ is the area covered by the rectangle bin. If we keep decreasing the interval to a very small values the with at exact $t$ min will be so small that area will be negligible i.e, $P(\text{exact t min})=0$ .

![](assets/cont.%20prob/pdf%20ex2.1.svg)

Hence for a continuous distribution, probability is defined in intervals and the area under the curve in intervals is the probability
