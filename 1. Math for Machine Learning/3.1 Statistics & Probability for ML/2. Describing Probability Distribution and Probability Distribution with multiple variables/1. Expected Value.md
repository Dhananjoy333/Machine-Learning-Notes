The **expected value** (also called the **mean** or **expectation**) is the long-run average outcome of a random variable if an experiment is repeated many times.

Example - 2 : If we play a game where every time in a coin toss we get head we win 10 and if we lose we gain nothing. To play the game we have to pay certain amount of money at the beginning, What is the maximum amount of money we should be paying at beginning to play the game?

![](../assets/expected%20val/expected%20val%20ex-2.svg)

Here the both head and tail has equal chances of appearing. Now if because the chances are equal if we play the game for a long period of time we can expect that by the end equal amount of heads and equal amount of tails will appear. By calculating the win cost we can say that on average in a single game we expect to win 5 . Hence paying 6 to play the game is not wise. If we play the game by initially paying 5 atleast in long run we can expect that we will gain 5 each time hence not loosing anything.

Remember : we are not saying that each time the coin is toss we will win 5 (we can't win 5 it is not even an option its either 10 or 0). Rather by saying "expect to win 5" we meant after playing the game for a long period of time if we calculate the statistical average i.e, 

$$
	\frac{\text{amount of price we won}}{\text{total no. of times game played}}
$$

The value obtained will be very close to 5 which we can interpret as winning 5 in each round.

Example - 3 : In a 3 coin toss game, for each head that appear we win $1. What is the maximum amount of money we should be willing to pay each time to play the game?

![](../assets/expected%20val/expected%20val%20ex-3.svg)

Here we have 3 conditions for win :
- 1 head will result in winning $1.
- 2 head will result in winning $2.
- 3 head will result in winning $3.
Looking at the possible outcomes we have :
- The probability of 1 head appearing is $\frac{3}{8}$ .
- The probability of 2 head appearing is $\frac{3}{8}$ .
- The probability of 3 head appearing is $\frac{1}{8}$ .
The weighted sum with the win price and probability will give the expected price of $1.5 i.e., if we play the game for a long period of time and find statistical mean of win we get that on average we will close to 1.5 each match. So to not lose we should be willing to pay at max $1.5 to start each round of the game.
