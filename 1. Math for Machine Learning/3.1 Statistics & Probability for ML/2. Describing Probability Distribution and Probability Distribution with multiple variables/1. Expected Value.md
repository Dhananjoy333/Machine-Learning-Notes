The **expected value** (also called the **mean** or **expectation**) is the long-run average outcome of a random variable if an experiment is repeated many times.

Here calculation of mean can be written in form of weighted probability sum, which also gives mean. In our case mean and expected value is the same.

Example - 2 : If we play a game where every time in a coin toss we get head we win 10 and if we lose we gain nothing. To play the game we have to pay certain amount of money at the beginning, What is the maximum amount of money we should be paying at beginning to play the game?

![](../assets/expected%20val/expected%20val%20ex-2.svg)

Here the both head and tail has equal chances of appearing. Now if because the chances are equal if we play the game for a long period of time we can expect that by the end equal amount of heads and equal amount of tails will appear. By calculating the win cost we can say that on average in a single game we expect to win 5 . Hence paying 6 to play the game is not wise. If we play the game by initially paying 5 atleast in long run we can expect that we will gain 5 each time hence not loosing anything.

Remember : we are not saying that each time the coin is toss we will win 5 (we can't win 5 it is not even an option its either 10 or 0). Rather by saying "expect to win 5" we meant after playing the game for a long period of time if we calculate the statistical average i.e, 

$$
	\frac{\text{amount of price we won}}{\text{total no. of times game played}}
$$

The value obtained will be very close to 5 which we can interpret as winning 5 in each round.

Example - 3 : In a 3 coin toss game, for each head that appear we win $1. What is the maximum amount of money we should be willing to pay each time to play the game?

![](../assets/expected%20val/expected%20val%20ex-3.svg)

Here we have 3 conditions for win :
- 1 head will result in winning $1.
- 2 head will result in winning $2.
- 3 head will result in winning $3.
Looking at the possible outcomes we have :
- The probability of 1 head appearing is $\frac{3}{8}$ .
- The probability of 2 head appearing is $\frac{3}{8}$ .
- The probability of 3 head appearing is $\frac{1}{8}$ .
The weighted sum with the win price and probability will give the expected price of $1.5 i.e., if we play the game for a long period of time and find statistical mean of win we get that on average we will close to 1.5 each match. So to not lose we should be willing to pay at max $1.5 to start each round of the game.

## Expected value in Continuous distribution

### Uniform distribution

![](../assets/expected%20val/uniform%20dist%20ex.jpg)

Here because the total area is 1 and the rectangle is 10 unit long. The probability at all interval will be $\frac{1}{10}$. Now we know for continuous distribution the random variable is not a discrete value rather it is an interval and to compute expectation we need discrete random value as 

$$
	\mathbb{E}[X]=\sum_{x}xP_{x}(x)
$$

But in continuous distribution for a discrete value

$$
	P(X=x)=0
$$

Looking at the above uniform distribution, we can say each interval contribute $\frac{1}{10}$ probability. Now we divide the distribution into very tiny intervals and calculate the expectation lets say for 2 and 2.001.

$$
	\begin{align}
	P(2\leq X\leq 2.001)&\approx f_{X}(2)\Delta x \\
	\text{we know, here all interval has probability of }\frac{1}{10} \\
	\text{So,} \\
	f_{x}(2)&=\frac{1}{10} \\
	P(2\leq X\leq 2.001)&\approx \frac{1}{10}(0.001) \\
	P(2\leq X\leq 2.001)&\approx 0.0001 \\
	\text{Now the contribution of this tiny interval to expectation is} \\
	&=x.P \\
	&=2.(0.0001) \\
	&=0.0002 \\
	\text{We do this for every tiny interval} \\
	\mathbb{E}[X]&\approx \sum_{x}xf_{X}(x)\Delta x \\
	\text{If the intervals are infinitely tiny the summation becomes an integral} \\
	\mathbb{E}[X]&= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} xf_{X}(x) \, dx  \\
	\end{align}
$$


![](../assets/expected%20val/cont%20expected%20val.svg)

For uniform distribution, because $f_{X}(x)$ is same in all intervals and total probability is 1. Finding expectation is simply calculating the average of $x-axis$ .

## Skewed Distribution

In discrete distribution we saw if all cases have equally likely probability then excepted value is mean of random variable. However if the probability is uneven the expected value shifts towards larger value to balance out both side.

![](../assets/expected%20val/discrete%20ex.svg)

In continuous distribution if we take the same example as that of call expectation around certain time period. Here idea remains the same the expected value is simply a balancing point of

## Other measures of Central Tendency : Median and Mode

![](../assets/expected%20val/mean%20disadvantage.svg)

If we simply look at the average one would think all graduate of University of North Carolina earns $250,000 but this is not the case. Only 1 person (Michael Jordan) earned so high salary that he skewed the University average to very high amount. This is the disadvantage of mean, it is highly sensitive to outliers.
### Median

Median is the point which divide the distribution into equal parts i.e, 
- 50% of distribution is above median. 
- 50% of distribution below median. 
### Mode

Mode is the point where distribution has highest probability
