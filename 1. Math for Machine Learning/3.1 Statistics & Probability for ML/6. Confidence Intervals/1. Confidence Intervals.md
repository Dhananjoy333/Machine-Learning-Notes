A confidence interval is a range of values, calculated from sample data, that is likely to contain the true value of an unknown population parameter. Instead of guessing a single number, it provides a range (like 160–170 cm) with a set level of certainty, usually 95%.

If we try to calculate the mean from different sample space it is highly unlikely tat we will get the exact mean of population.

![](../assets/Confidence%20Intervals/height%20ex.svg)

Hence we use certain interval to determine if the population mean can exist under certain range with certainty.

![](../assets/Confidence%20Intervals/height%20ex_1.svg)

Example 1:
If we are moving in a road and a key falls on the way, if we try to search for the key, first we will look at the place where the key most likely dropped then move left or right from the initial position

![](../assets/Confidence%20Intervals/key%20ex.svg)

A **confidence level** is the statistical probability that a chosen range of values will capture the true value of a population parameter if an experiment or survey is repeated many times. It is usually shown as a percentage, such as 95%.

![](../assets/Confidence%20Intervals/key%20ex_1.svg)

Example 2:
The actual population mean is unknown to us. So we take the mean of sample and create a confidence interval. Confidence interval is basically our sample mean and some margin of error (i.e., left and right movement)

$$
	\bar{x}\pm \text{margin of error}
$$
The level of significance (denoted as α or alpha) is the risk level set before a study begins. $\alpha=0.05$ mean 5% of the probability area is outside my confidence interval. And because we're making a two-sided interval, that 5% is split equally on the two ends of the distribution: 2.5%. 

![](../assets/Confidence%20Intervals/height%20mean%20ex.svg)

If we take a sample of 1 person and create the confidence interval. And repeat the same for as many people as possible and mark the intervals that contain the actual population mean (unknown to us) as green and those whose interval does not contain as red

![](../assets/Confidence%20Intervals/confidence%20level.svg)

This is what 95% confidence level mean. It means if I repeatedly take samples and construct a confidence interval each time, about 95% of those intervals will contain the actual mean.
## Changing the interval

### Changing the number of sample taken

![](../assets/Confidence%20Intervals/interval.svg)

- If we take the mean of multiple sample means the value becomes equal to the population mean.
- However for standard deviation if the number of sample is increased the standard deviation of sample mean decreases by $\sqrt{ n }$. Which means if we increase the number of samples the normal distribution curve will be narrower.

![](../assets/Confidence%20Intervals/interval_1.svg)

Here we can see if number of sample taken is increased for each mean calculation. For the same confidence level the we have more precise estimate for the true value of $\mu$ .
### Changing the confidence level

![](../assets/Confidence%20Intervals/interval_2.svg)

Changing the confidence level mean changing the margin of error or if we create confidence interval multiple times for a given sample, the chances of actual mean existing in those confidence interval is changed. Here we can see decreasing the confidence level decreases probability of actual mean existing on the confidence intervals. In real world case we should never decrease the confidence level below 90% (95% being ideal) instead we should aim to increase the number of sample taken for each confidence interval calculation.

## Margin of Error

 Confidence interval is given by $\bar{x}\pm \text{margin of error}$ . $\bar{x}$ is our sample mean, now we need to find margin of error for various confidence level.

We represent the height of population with a normal distribution. Now we take $n$ no. of samples and find mean multiple times. We know from Central Limit Theorem (CLT) when we take $n$ no. of sample and find mean multiple times it always form normal distribution. Here the black curve is the distribution of multiple means where spread is controlled by no. of observation in each sample taken. 

![](../assets/Confidence%20Intervals/margin%20cal.svg)

We also know for a normal distribution it follow the rule of $68-95-99$ Rule which suggest :
- In a normal distribution 66% of data are in 1 standard deviation and 95% of data is in 2 standard deviation and 99% in 3 standard deviation

![](../assets/Confidence%20Intervals/margin%20cal_1.svg)

If we standardize the data 68% of data lie in range of -1 to 1 and 95% of data lie in range of -2 to 2. This values are actually approximated in reality 95% of rate lie in range of -1.96 to 1.96
