## Uniform Distribution

A uniform distribution is a type of probability model where every single outcome in a specific range has the exact same chance of happening.

For ex : You call a tech support line. They can answer any time between zero and 15 min and if they don't answer in this time, the line is disconnected.

![](../assets/cont.%20dist/uniform%20dist%20ex.svg)

If we note the last 200 calls made and made the graph w.r.t. time. We notice there is no particular pattern. At every point in time there is an equally likely chance for the call to come.

Now that we know every time interval has equal probability of call coming the PDF graph will be a rectangular block with 15 separation. Question is how high will be each block (or height of the rectangle). We already know that area under the rectangle must be 1. Hence if total width is 15 height must be $\frac{1}{15}$.

## Normal Distribution/Gaussian Distribution

A normal distribution, also called a bell curve or Gaussian distribution, is a data pattern where most values cluster around a central average. The graph is symmetric, meaning the left and right sides match. Values near the middle happen most often, while values far from the middle happen less. 

Normal distribution is one of the most important distribution because when we take large number of dataset or do experiment multiple times, the data usually starts to form bell curve  i.e, around certain mean value, most values show up and few extremes.

![](../assets/cont.%20dist/normalturqa.jpg)

### Formula of Normal Distribution

Since the datas always from a bell curve there must be some equation that can form the same curve as that of datas.

We notice an equation 

$$
	e^{-\frac{x^2}{2}}
	
$$
makes a bell curve.

- Fitting mean : We take a random dataset that makes a bell shape and try to align the mean of the equation to mean of data graph.

![](../assets/cont.%20dist/bell%20shaped%20-1.svg)

- Fitting variance : Above we notice the thickness of the equation is thinner than graph. To fix this we can add variance.

![](../assets/cont.%20dist/bell%20shaped%20-2.svg)

- Fitting Height ; We again notice the height of eq is taller than that of data graph. To fit this we look at the area under the curve, for the data graph we know all probability must sum up to 1 hence area of data graph is 1. Now we find the area of the eq. by integration and simply divide the area with the eq to get value of 1.

![](../assets/cont.%20dist/bell%20shaped-3.svg)

In general

![](../assets/cont.%20dist/normal%20dist%20eq.svg)

Here we notice the equation is controlled entirely by mean and variance,
- Mean $\mu$ controls the center of the distribution.
- Variance $\sigma$ controls the spread of the distribution. Or how much on average each individual data deviate from mean.
If we have a random variable with PDF being $\frac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{ 2\pi }}e^{-1/2((x-\mu)/\sigma)^2}$ , i.e., random variable follows a normal distribution we can represent it as

$$
	X \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)
$$

### Standard Normal Distribution

When the value of mean is 0 and standard deviation is 1 it is called standard normal distribution

![](../assets/cont.%20dist/SND.svg)

Having the values of mean and standard deviation as 0 and 1 respectively, making any operation of function $f_{X}(x)$ w.r.t x much easier. Hence we real cases, if data form normal distribution we usually shift the datapoint to make the mean 0 and standard deviation 1. This is called ***Standardization***.

### Standardization

If we have a dataset whose mean is 2 and standard deviation 2.5, we can move the mean to 0 by simply subtracting it and  to make standard deviation 1 we can divide the value
