### Mean

-> Mean is the measure of average of all values of sample set.

- Important Notations

| Population                | Sample               |
| ------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Count = $N$               | Count = $n$          |
| Mean = $\mu$              | Mean = $\bar{x}$     |
| Variance = $\sigma^2$     | Variance = $S^2$     |
| Std. deviation = $\sigma$ | Std. deviation = $S$ |
-> The general formula for the mean (the mathematical average) is calculated by adding all the data values together and dividing that total by the number of values in the dataset.
$$
	\begin{align}
	\bar{x} &= \frac{x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}\dots x_{n}}{n} \\
	 \\
	&=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^nx_{i}}{n}
	\end{align}
$$
-> Mean can be descriptive and inferential based on the type of data we are looking.
Example : If we want to find the average math marks of class 7 students in xyz school ?
- Here let class 7 of xyz school has 120 students. So, all 120 students is our population.
- Case 1 : we go to each one of the 120 students, ask their marks and find average ($\mu$). The value of average is descriptive as it describe the average mark of 120 students.
- Case 2 : we go to 40 students and ask their mark and find average ($\bar{x}$). Lets say we get $\bar{x}=70$. Then we assume all 120 students will have marks around 70. This is inferential.

-> Mean is highly sensitive to outliers (outliers are values which are very different (too high or too low) from all other values).
For example : What is the average CTC in LPA in mechanical and computer science branch of engineering in a college . Lets analyze for 8 students : 
- Mechanical branch : Lets say in Mechanical branch packages obtained in LPA is $[5, 8, 9, 12, 4, 7, 6, 9]$
$$
	Mean(\bar{x}) = \frac{5+8+9+12+4+7+6+9}{8}=7.5
$$
- CS branch : packages obtained in CS branch $[5, 9, 14, 12, 9, 8, 7, 120]$. Here 1 students got a packages of 1.2 crores .
$$
	Mean(\bar{x})= \frac{5+9+14+12+9+8+7+120}{8}=23
$$
- Clearly by looking in CS the average is not 23 but due to 1 students getting really high CTC its pulling the mean towards itself.

### Median

-> Median is the middle value in a list of sorted numbers.
- If n = odd no., we will have 1 middle no. which is our median.
- if n = even no., we will have 2 middle no.. So average of those 2 number is median.

-> Median is a robust measure of central tendency. It is not affected by outliers.
For example : What is the median CTC in LPA in mechanical and computer science branch of engineering in a college . Lets analyze for 9 students : 
- Mechanical branch : Lets say in Mechanical branch packages obtained in LPA is $[5, 8, 9, 12, 4, 7, 6, 9, 13]$. First we sort it $[4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 9, 12, 13]$
$$
	Median= \frac{9+1}{2}=\text{5th position} = 8
$$
- CS branch : packages obtained in CS branch $[5, 9, 14, 12, 9, 8, 7, 6, 120]$. Here 1 students got a packages of 1.2 crores . If we sort it $[5, 6, 7, 8, 9 , 9, 12, 14, 120]$
$$
	Median= \frac{9+1}{2}=\text{5th position} = 9
$$
- Clearly the outlier has no effect on median as median does not look at the actual values it just based on position of values. 

### Mode

-> Mode is the value that appears most frequently in a dataset.  
-> May have more than 1 mode value.  
-> If no number repeats , there is no mode.  
-> Unaffected by extreme values (outliers).  
-> useful for categorical data.  
Example :
- $[2, 3, 3, 4, 7]$ --> 3
- $[4, 4, 5, 6, 6, 7]$ --> 4 and 6
- $[1, 2, 3, 4, 5]$ --> no mode.
