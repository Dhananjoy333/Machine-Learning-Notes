People say derivative is "instantaneous rate of change". But when we think about it the statement is an oxymoron, instantaneous means at one point in time i.e., time is stopped for that instant whereas change is something which is measured over a course of time i.e., if time is stopped there is no change.

![](car.svg)

Let us imagine a car moving in a straight line, the car starts slow from point $A$ then speeds up and by the end point $B$ it slows back down. The car take 10 sec to reach from point $A$ to $B$ and travels a distance of 100 m. we can graph this motion by taking y-axis as distance and x-axis as time.

- Distance vs Time Graph $s(t)$

![](car%20d-t%20graph.svg)

Notice, the graph is not straight as the speed of car changes. Initially car starts slowly so it travel less distance between two time interval but as car speed increases its distance travelled between two time intervals increases showing increasing in steepness of the curve and by the end the car again slows down. Here it shows, at certain interval of time how much distance the car is covering.

Now if we add velocity graph to it

- Velocity vs Time Graph $v(t)$

![](car%20velocity%20graph.svg)

Here if we see the velocity graph initially the car start slowing and start to speed up showing in rive of graph, then after max velocity car starts to slow down, showing downward graph, then it comes to rest i.e., 0 velocity. 

![](other%20s-t%20and%20v-t%20graphs.svg)

here we see that change in distance vs time graph, changes the velocity vs time graph. Hence there is a relation between the two. Seeing the graph we notice at steeper slope of $s(t)$ , the $v(t)$ increases.

Hence velocity is dependent on slope of $s(t)$ , now slope can only exist between two interval, slope has no meaning at single point. So asking what is velocity of car at an instant of time by taking snapshot of the car at that instant is meaningless, as snapshot is basically a point on graph and we need to snapshot to compare and evaluate the velocity.

![](velocity.svg)

But in our previous graphs of velocity-time graph  we are showing a velocity value for single point in time , but velocity cannot exist for a single point in time (velocity need change in time) it need two points , this creates a paradox.

![](velocity%20paradox.svg)

## How do we solve this ?

In real life speedometer shows the velocity of the car. Speedometer keeps track of time and distance travelled between two very small interval and calculate velocity with those. Hence speedometer does not calculate "Instantaneous rate of change" it calculates "over a small interval of time rate of change" 

![](speedometer.svg)

If we call that small change in distance as $ds$ and small change in time as $dt$. So,
$$
	v = \frac{ds}{dt}
$$

![](velocity%20as%20function.svg)

Hence to find slope we can find ratio of $\frac{ds}{dt}$ . Now $\frac{ds}{dt}$ can be calculated for any given point in time. So, $\frac{ds}{dt}$ is a function of time (velocity as a function of time). Hence when we showed the graph the velocity values of graph is actually calculated over a time change in in time.
$$
	\begin{array}{c}
	dt=0.01 \\
	\frac{ds}{dt}(t) = \frac{s(t + dt) - s(t)}{dt}
	\end{array}
$$
This change in $s$ for small change in $t$ i.e., $\frac{ds}{dt}$ is the idea behind derivative. But in pure math, derivative is not the value of ratio of $\frac{ds}{dt}$ at certain time change in time $dt$ like 0.01, 0.02, 0.5 etc. Instead, derivative is the value of ratio of $\frac{ds}{dt}$ as the value of $dt$ approaches 0 but never reaching it i.e., $dt$ = 0.00000......1. 

![](derivative.svg)

we know $\frac{ds}{dt}$ shows the slope of line passing through two different points, as the $dt$ approaches 0 (derivative) the slope  of the line approaches the slope of the line tangent to point t. Hence derivative in pure math, give us slope of a line, tangent to a point. Note, $dt$ is not infinitely small nor is $dt=0$ . $dt$ is a finite value it which approaches 0.

![](find%20slope.svg)

Here we have a distance-time graph where distance is shown as a function of time $s(t)=t^3$ . Now if we try to find the derivative at time 2 sec

![](derivative-eq.svg)

we understand that derivative seem like a complicated idea of increasing something by a very small amount $ds$ so small that we can't even write it $ds=0.00000......1$ . But Here when we find a derivative of a function it gives us a simple nice function with which we can find value of slope (derivative) at any point of time t. 

The main reason is that in derivative we don't have a fixed $dt$ value if we knew the $dt$ value we would need to actually solve the complex equation but because in derivative $dt$ value is so small we can ignore some complexity of the equation giving us simpler solution.
