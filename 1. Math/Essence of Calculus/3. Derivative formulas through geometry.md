Let us take a simple function of $x^2$

![](assets/derivative%20formula%20with%20geometry/xsquare-graph.svg)

we can easily solve its derivative, but what does this look like geometrically $x^2$ can be seen as area under square with side x

![](assets/derivative%20formula%20with%20geometry/area%20change.svg)

Here sides are increased by small amount of $dx$. Hence derivative geometrically tell us how much the area increase for small unit increment in sides.

Similarly, for $x^3$ 

![](assets/derivative%20formula%20with%20geometry/x%20cube.svg)

This means derivative of $x^3$ which is the rate at which $x^3$ changes per unit change in x is $3x^2$.

>[!Note]
>General equation,
>$$
>	\frac{d(x^n)}{dx}=nx^{n-1}
>$$
>This is called "Power Rule".

### Derivative of $\sin(\theta)$

![](assets/derivative%20formula%20with%20geometry/sine.svg)

We can understand $\sin(\theta)$ with the help of a circle having unit radius if we move the radius around the circle for lets say 0.8 length the angle that we get is 0.8 radian. The perpendicular line from the end of radius to the x-axis is out $\sin(\theta)$ value. 

![](assets/derivative%20formula%20with%20geometry/mving%20radius.svg)

Now if we increase the angle the value of $\sin(\theta)$ move up and down between -1 and 1. Now when we draw the graph of value of $\sin(\theta)$ :

![](assets/derivative%20formula%20with%20geometry/sine%20graph.svg)

we get a wave that move up and down between -1 and 1. By now we know derivative tells us the slope of graph at a point. Hence if we draw the graph of derivative of $\sin(\theta)$ it will look like :

![](assets/derivative%20formula%20with%20geometry/derivative%20of%20sine.svg)

Initially the slope is 1 , as we move forward slope decreases and reaches 0. Then the slope become negative and later become 0 , again the slope become 0 and as we move forward it repeats.

Now if we look closer the graph formed by the derivative look exactly like $\cos(\theta)$ graph . Hence,
$$
	\frac{d\sin(\theta)}{d(\theta)}=\cos(\theta)
$$
