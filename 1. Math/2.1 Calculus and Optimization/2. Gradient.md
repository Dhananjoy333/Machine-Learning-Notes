## Tangent Plane

![](assets/gradient/2D%20and%203D%20tangent.svg)

For equation with one variable whose graph exist in 2D plane we can define a tangent by a line. But if equation is made of 2 or more variable the tangent becomes more complex. Here if equation is made of 2 variable ($f(x,y),z=x^2+y^2$) in 3D space then at any point $(x,y,z) or (x,y,x^2+y^2)$ the tangent formed is a plane. Unlike graph in 2D plane where eq. of tangent can be found by differentiation, here in 3D we need to do ***Partial Differentiation*** as the output $z$ is dependent on 2 variables $x$ and $y$.

## Partial Derivative

To do partial derivative of an equation we differentiate using one variable and keeping all other constant. For eq $f(x,y)=x^2+y^2$.   
Initially we treat $y$ as constant. 

![](assets/gradient/y%20constant.svg)

This can be imagined as cutting the 3D object at certain y value with a sheet of paper and the outlines of the object is seen on the paper giving a 2D parabola whose derivative we can find
$$
\begin{align}
f(x,y) &= x^2+y^2 \\
\frac{df}{dx}&=2x \text{ [ y is constant, we know derivative of constant is 0.]}
\end{align}
$$
Now we treat as constant and differentiate w.r.t $y$

![](assets/gradient/x%20constant.svg)
$$
	\begin{align}
	f(x,y) &= x^2+y^2 \\
\frac{df}{dy}&=2y \text{ [ x is constant, we know derivative of constant is 0.]}
	\end{align}
$$
The plane formed by the lines when differentiation w.r.t $x$ and $y$ is our tangent plane on which infinitely many tangent lie for a point
![](assets/gradient/tangent%20plane.svg)

## Gradient

When we partially differentiate a multivariable equation and write the partial derivatives in vector form it is called gradient.
$$
	\begin{align}
	Gradient &= \begin{bmatrix}
	\frac{df}{dx}\\ 
	 \frac{df}{dy} \\
	 \dots
	\end{bmatrix} \\
	\text{Example,} \\
	f(x,y)&= x^2 + y^2 \\
	\frac{df}{dx}&=2x \\
	\frac{dy}{dy}&=2y \\
	Gradient&= \begin{bmatrix}
	2x \\
	2y
	\end{bmatrix}
	\end{align}
$$
## Min/Max in two variable

![](assets/gradient/min-max%20in%203D.svg)

Similar to how we can find minima or maxima in one variable by differentiating the function and equating it to 0. In multivariant equation we simply do partial differentiation and equation each to 0.

![](assets/gradient/multivariant%20min.svg)

Solving a variable require lot of computation : finding individual partial derivative, solving for each equation etc. Normally in machine learning we have 100+ variables now doing partial derivative and solving for each individual variable is very expensive computationally. So, instead of partial derivative method we need other method to find min/max. This other method is called "Gradient Descent".
