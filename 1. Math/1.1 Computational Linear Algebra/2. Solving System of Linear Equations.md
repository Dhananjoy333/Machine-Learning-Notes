## Singularity

In a **linear system**, **singularity** refers to whether the **coefficient matrix** is invertible or not.
Suppose you have a system of equations:
$$
	\begin{align}
	Ax&=B \\
	where,  \\
	A&= \text{coefficient matrix} \\
	x&= \text{vector of unknowns} \\
	B&=\text{vector of constants}
	\end{align}
$$
### Non-singular matrix

- The determinant of $A$ is **not zero**.
- $A$ has an inverse $A^-1$.
- The system has **exactly one unique solution**.
$$
	\begin{align}
	2x+y&=5 \\
	x-y&=1 \\
	\text{Matrix A} \\
	&\begin{bmatrix}
	2&1 \\
	1&-1
	\end{bmatrix} \\
	\det(A)&=2(-1)-1(1) \\
	&=-3\neq0
	\end{align}
$$

### Singular matrix

- The determinant of $A$ is **zero**.
- $A$ has **no inverse**.
- A singular system **does not guarantee a unique solution**. It can have:
    - **Infinitely many solutions** (consistent)
    - **No solution** (inconsistent)
#### Infinitely Many Solutions
$$
	\begin{align}
	x+y&=2 \\
	2x+2y&=4 \\
	\text{Matrix A} \\
	&\begin{bmatrix}
	1&1 \\
	2&2
	\end{bmatrix} \\
	\det(A)&=1(2)-1(2) \\
	&=0
	\end{align}
$$
- Here second equation is just twice of first so infinitely many solutions.
#### No solution
$$
	\begin{align}
	x+y&=2 \\
	2x+2y&=5 \\
	\text{Matrix A} \\
	&\begin{bmatrix}
	1&1 \\
	2&2
	\end{bmatrix} \\
	\det(A)&=1(2)-1(2) \\
	&=0
	\end{align}
$$
- Here even though the determinant is 0 the constant does not match the coefficient relation i.e., coefficients of variable are just twice but constant is not.

## Gaussian Elimination

The **Row Echelon Form (REF)** method (also called **Gaussian Elimination**) is one of the most important ways to solve a system of linear equations. This helps in finding the values of unknowns and also give information about rank of linear system. The goal is to transform the matrix into a staircase shape, then solve from the bottom upward. 
$$
	\begin{bmatrix}
	*&*&* \\
	0&*&* \\
	0&0&* \\
	\end{bmatrix}
$$
Operations allowed on rows when creating echelon form are :
1. Swap two rows.
$$
	R_{1}\leftrightarrow R_{2}
$$
2. Multiply a row by a non-zero constant.
$$
	R_{1}=3R_{2}
$$
3. Add (or subtract) a multiple of one row to another
$$
	R_{3}=R_{3}-2R_{1}
$$
### Solving eq. using Gaussian Elimination

Eq. :
$$
\begin{align}
x+2y+z&=9 \\
2x+3y+4z&=20 \\
3x+y+3z&=14
\end{align}
$$

#### Step-1 : Create the augmented matrix
$$
	\left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & 1 & 9 \\ 2& 3 & 4 & 20 \\ 3 & 1 & 3 & 14 \end{array} \right]
$$
- Here our top left is already 1 so no operation need if it was not. Simply divide the whole row with the top left value or simply swap with any row that has 1.

### Step-2 : Turn values below pivot point to 0.

- Here pivot points are the values in main diagonal (top left to bottom right). 
$$
\begin{align} \\
	&\left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & 1 & 9 \\ 2& 3 & 4 & 20 \\ 3 & 1 & 3 & 14 \end{array} \right] \\ \\
\text{Eliminate row 2,} \\
	R_{2}&=R_{2}-2R_{1}, \\
	&=\left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c}2 & 3 & 4 & 20 \end{array} \right]-2\left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c}1 & 2 & 1 & 9 \end{array} \right] \\
	&=\left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c}0 & -1 & 2 & 2 \end{array} \right] \\
	& \\
\text{Eliminate row 3,} \\
R_{3}&=R_{3}-3R_{1} \\
&=\left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c}3 & 1 & 3 & 14 \end{array} \right]-3\left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1& 2 & 1 & 9 \end{array} \right] \\
	&=\left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c}0 & -5 & 0 & -13 \end{array} \right] \\ \\
\text{Matrix formed,} \\
&\left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & 1 & 9 \\ 0& -1 & 2 & 2 \\ 0 & -5 & 0 & -13 \end{array} \right] \\
& \\
\text{Solving for next pivot i.e, -1,} \\
R_{3}&=R_{3}-5R_{2} \\
&=\left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c}0 & -5 & 0 & -13 \end{array} \right]-5\left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 0& -1 & 2 & 2 \end{array} \right] \\
	&=\left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c}0 & 0 & -10 & -23 \end{array} \right] \\
	& \\
\text{Final echelon form,} \\
&\left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & 1 & 9 \\ 0& -1 & 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & -10 & -23 \end{array} \right]
\end{align}	
$$

#### Step-4 : Back Substitution 

- Solve the equation by substituting the values from last row.
$$
	\begin{align} \\
	\text{Third row,} \\
	
	-10z&=-23 \\
	z&=\frac{23}{10}=2.3 \\ \\
	
	\text{Second row,} \\
	-1y+2z&=2 \\
	-y+4.6&=2 \\
	y&=2.6 \\ \\
	\text{First row,} \\
	1x+2y+1z&=9 \\
	x+5.2+2.3&=9 \\
	x&=1.5
	
	\end{align}
$$

## Gauss Jordan Elimination

The Reduced Row Echelon Form (RREF) is a variation of REF here instead of doing back substitution we continue to conduct the row operation until we get ***Identity Matrix***.
$$
	\begin{bmatrix}
	1&0&0 \\
	0&1&0 \\
	0&0&1
	\end{bmatrix}
$$
This way the remaining constant values are the values of unknown variables.

For a system of linear equations if we conduct REF and achieve
$$
	\begin{align}
	&\left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 2 & 1 & 9 \\ 0& -1 & 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 5 & 15 \end{array} \right]
	\end{align}
$$
Now we keep doing the row operations until we achieve
$$
	\begin{align}
	&\left[ \begin{array}{ccc|c} 1 & 0 & 0 & -2 \\ 0& 1 & 0 & 4 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 3 \end{array} \right]
	\end{align}
$$
Now we can easily say
$$
\begin{align}
x&=-2 \\
y&=4 \\
z&=3
\end{align}
$$
## Finding Rank 

To find the rank of linear system we do the Gaussian Elimination for the given system.   

Case 1 : For a $3*3$ matrix if all rows have unique values then its Rank 3
$$
	\begin{bmatrix}
	1&2&3 \\
	0&4&5 \\
	0&0&6
	\end{bmatrix}
$$

Case 2 : If one of the rows all become 0 or one row is copy of other than it is Rank 2.
$$
	\begin{bmatrix}
	1&2&3 \\
	0&4&5 \\
	0&0&0
	\end{bmatrix}
	\text{OR}
	\begin{bmatrix}
	1&2&3 \\
	0&4&5 \\
	0&4&5 \\
	\end{bmatrix}
	\text{OR any one row is a multiple of other}
$$

Case 3 : If two rows become all 0 or all rows are of same value or the other two row is a multiple of one row.
$$
	\begin{bmatrix}
	1&2&3 \\
	0&0&0 \\
	0&0&0
	\end{bmatrix}
	\text{OR}
	\begin{bmatrix}
	1&2&3 \\
	n.1&n.2&n.3 \\
	m.1&m.2&m.3 \\
	\end{bmatrix}
$$

Rank gives us information about number of independent equations.

## Degree of Freedom

Degrees of freedom tell you **how many variables are free to take any value**.  
For a consistent system,
$$
	\text{Degree of Freedom = Number of variables - Rank}
$$
This is because each independent equation removes one degree of freedom.
