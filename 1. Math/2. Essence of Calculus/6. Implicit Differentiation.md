The equation of circle has both x and y involved in it, Unlike normal functions where y = some function of x , Here as both x and y are involved it is called implicit function. We cant directly differentiate the function w.r.t x.

## Solving the issue

![](assets/Implicit%20Differentiation/ladder-problem.svg)

here we have a 5m ladder which is standing at 4m from ground and base is 3m far. If the ladder starts falling at 1m/s what is the rate at which the base of ladder is moving away from the wall.

![](assets/Implicit%20Differentiation/ladder-sol1.svg)

Here the x(t) and y(t) are bounded by the Pythagorean theorem at every point in time it will always satisfy the Pythagoras theorem .

Here we could try to differentiate x with respect to t which would work but required multiple chain rule. But there is a simpler way to solve this

![](assets/Implicit%20Differentiation/ladder-sol2.svg)

Here as both x and y are bounded by time t i.e., as time pass y decrease and x increase. At no point in time there will be a point where y decrease but x does not, because if that happen Pythagoras theorem will be broken which it must not. So we differentiate both w.r.t time t. Now $\frac{dy}{dt}$ tells for small change in time t how does y changes which we already know as y is decreasing at the rate of -1m/s and $\frac{dx}{dt}$ tells for small change in t how x changes which is what we want applying this we can find $\frac{dx}{dt}$ . 

## Back to Circle

In circle the problem is we will nudge the x and y by tiny amount $dy$ and $dx$ but they are not bounded by anything like time in previous case. To solve this :

![](assets/Implicit%20Differentiation/circle-func-der.svg)

Now lets assume S as a function of x and y i.e., it takes random x and y values and gives out some number by  equation of $x^2+y^2$ like if x=1, y=2 then S=5; x=3, y=8, S=53 etc. Now we have a point x = 3 and y = 4 so S= 25. We nudge x and y by small amount $dx$ and $dy$. Now new point obtained is by solving for S($x+dx$, $y+dy$) and $dS$ is simply the change in value of S when at position S(3,4) to S($x+dx$, $y+dy$).

![](assets/Implicit%20Differentiation/dS.svg)

Now for circle we don't take random values of x and y, we choose value of x and y such that the sum of square remain same in our case it is 25. Hence we take all x and y values whose sum of square is 25. Now when we nudge the values of x and y with $ds$ and $dy$ we do it in such a way that $(x+dx)^2+(y+dy)^2$ also equal to 25. Now $S_{new}$ - $S$ is basically 25-25 which is 0. And finally resolving the other side :

$$
	\begin{align}
	2xdx+2ydy&=0 \\
	xdx+ydy&=0 \\
	ydy&=-xdx \\
	\frac{dy}{dx}&=-\frac{x}{y}
	\end{align}
$$

Hence we have differentiated an implicit function

![](assets/Implicit%20Differentiation/Screenshot%20(744).png)

>[! Rule to differentiate Implicit functions]
>Whenever you differentiate a term containing yyy, **multiply by $\frac{dy}{dx}$** (Chain Rule)

Example 1 :

$$
	\begin{align}
	x^2+y^2&=25 \\
	2x+2y\frac{dy}{dx}&=0 \\
	\frac{dy}{dx}&=-\frac{x}{y}
	\end{align}
$$
Example 2 :

$$
\begin{align}
    x^2 + \sin(y)&=5\\
	2x + \cos(y)\frac{dy}{dx} &=0 \\
	\frac{dy}{dx}&=-\frac{2x}{\cos(y)}
\end{align}
$$
Example 3 :

$$
	\begin{align}
	xy&=10 \\
	x\frac{dy}{dx}+y&=0 \\
	\frac{dy}{dx}&=-\frac{y}{x}
	\end{align}
$$
