## Derivative of exponents

Let us take an example of $2^x$ , Here let x be time and the function gives the population of a city at time t.
$$
	\begin{array}{c}
	P(t)=2^t \\
	\text{Derivative of this function is,} \\
	\frac{dP}{dt} \\
	\text{Change in population with tiny change in time}
	\end{array}
$$
Lets see the growth over a difference of 1 

Day 3 to Day 4, Day 4 and Day 5, Day 5 to Day 6 :
$$
\begin{array}{c}
\text{Day 3}=8 \\
\text{Day 4}=16 \\
\text{Growth}=8 \\
\text{} \\
\text{Day 4}=16 \\
\text{Day 5}=32 \\
\text{Growth}=16 \\
\text{} \\
\text{Day 5}=32 \\
\text{Day 6}=64 \\
\text{Growth}=32
\end{array}
$$
It seems the rate of growth after a full day is same as whatever the population was at the start of the day.

So it seems 

$$
	\begin{array}{c}
	\frac{d(2^t)}{dt}=2^t \\
	\text{Idea is right but answer is wrong as we are considering rate of grwoth over full day but derivative wants t to be as small as possible like growth over 0.0000.....1 day}
	\end{array}
$$

![](assets/Euler%20number/expo.svg)

If we solve it we see that all $dt$ terms are separated by $2^t$ and if we try to find value of $\frac{2^{dt}-1}{dt}$ by putting smaller and smaller value it approaches to a constant number around 0.6931.... So if derivative of an exponent is the exponent itself multiplied by some constant value, can we find an exponents value such that the constant comes out to be 1 so that derivative of that exponent is exponent itself.

## Euler Number e

Euler number ($e=2.71828$) is a value where as dt approaches 0 the value of $\frac{e^{dt}-1}{dt}$ approaches to constant 1. Hence $e$ is a special number whose exponential derivative is the exponent itself
$$
	\frac{de^t}{dt}=e^t
$$
Hence we can easily find the value of all values which are proportional to $e$ like $e^{2t}$, $e^{3t}$ etc. All we do is apply the chain rule
$$
\begin{aligned}
	\frac{de^{3t}}{dt} &= e^{3t}\left( \frac{d(3t)}{dt} \right)\\
	&=3.e^{3t}
\end{aligned}
$$
If this works the same rule must also be followed by other values i.e., $2^t$ must have relation with $2^{2t}$, $2^{3t}$ etc. Here $2^{3t}$ is $8^t$ and if we look at the constants values the constant that we get by differentiating $8^t$ is approx. to constant in $2^t$. Hence we just need to know the mystery constant we have in derivative of $2^t$ and we can easily deduce derivative of number that are proportional to 2. Similar for 3 and other numbers.

## Logarithms (quick note)

If we have,
$$
	\begin{align}
	&y=2^x \\
	if,&x=1, y=2 \\
	 & x=2,y=4 \\
	 & x=3,y=8 \\
	 &\text{and so on...}
	\end{align}
$$
Here, $2^x$ is a function that take x and input and give y output. But if we reverse it and ask if y=4 what should be x value ?. Then this new function is represented with logarithms 
$$
\begin{array}{c}
\log_{2}(4)=x \\
\text{Here it ask what value should 2 be raised to, to get 4}
\end{array}
$$

In general terms it is written as

![](assets/Euler%20number/Logarithm.webp)

If the base is a constant value e (Euler number = 2.71828), then the $\log$ is written as $\ln$ .

$$
	\log_{e}(x)=\ln(x)
$$

Hence, $\ln$ ask to what power $e$ must be raised to get a value,

![](assets/Euler%20number/ln-e.svg)
